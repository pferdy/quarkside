James Clark, an MBA from the Cass Business school, reported his research to the <a href="http://www.linkedin.com/groups/Conservative-Technology-Forum-2652347?trk=myg_ugrp_ovr" target="_blank">Conservative Technology Forum</a> earlier this week. It was well researched, producing evidence, not just anecdote. First of all he smoked out two red herrings
<ol>
	<li> British culture is unsuited to entrepreneurs.</li>
	<li> Shortage of money for start-ups.</li>
</ol>
By looking at a the historical background of start-ups in the USA the was able to identify four drivers that we don't get right in the UK. Not only that, he gave some recommendations for policy consideration.
<ol>
	<li><strong>Culture</strong>:  Some aspects of our culture can act as “inhibitors”.  Policy should focus on enhancing cultural “activators”</li>
	<li> <strong>Knowledge and Experience</strong>: Not enough of the “right” kind of knowledge.  Policy should utilise local resources (people and industrial) whilst reducing barriers to importing talent</li>
	<li><strong>Finance</strong>: Financing gaps at crucial growth stages. Policy should target funding gaps and incentivise investment, but not crowd out private activity</li>
	<li><strong>Networks</strong>: Networks are stratified and information is poorly distributed Policy should target basic levels of information sharing and create “shared spaces” for start-ups.</li>
</ol>
The probing questions after the <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/nogoogle.ppt" target="_blank">presentation</a> were answered with great authority. Quarkside raised the issue of the <a title="Government sidelines SME Innovations" href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/11/02/government-sidelines-sme-innovations/" target="_blank">difficulties of innovative SMEs</a>  getting close to any procurement action in central government. The culture is wrong. But a noble Lord promised to take up the issue at ministerial levels. Let's see how it progresses.  Maybe <a title="Liam Maxwell: One year later" href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/10/31/liam-maxwell-one-year-later/" target="_blank">Liam Maxwell</a>, supremo for ICT Innovation in the Cabinet Office, can make a difference.
