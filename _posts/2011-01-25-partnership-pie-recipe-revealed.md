Independent review of performance and local accountability is needed to reduce the costs of internal public sector bureaucracy - or so suggests the <a href="http://bit.ly/fA2xVs" target="_blank">Centre for Public Service Partnerships</a>.   They responded to the <a href="http://bit.ly/dHJSTA" target="_blank">HM Treasury and Cabinet Office call for evidence on public service reform</a>.

Partnerships have to be the key to improving efficiency through shared and sharing services.  Quarkside hinted at this in November in relation to <a href="http://wp.me/p14MGf-9R" target="_blank">cooperation between the NHS and local authorities</a>.  A key enabler is a process for building adaptable and sustainable partnerships.  The groundwork for a viable multi-agency framework was completed in a <a href="http://bit.ly/9ZlimP" target="_blank">DCLG national project</a>.  There's also a <a href="http://bit.ly/brSoO3" target="_blank">roadmap</a> to use at the initial stages of building a partnership, showing partners the critical success factors and levels of risk.  It can be done in a standard (ie common) way across any (or all) recipe(s) for partnership pie.

The full response is worth a read, but the last point supports Quarkside's pressure for improving public sector governance:
<p style="padding-left:30px;"><em><strong>18. What specific data or information would you like to see made publicly available in order to help individuals and local communities hold services to account? </strong></em></p>
<p style="padding-left:30px;"><em><strong>There is a need for some standardised data for public service performance and costs which is comparable between agencies and services; verifiable; independently audited; and accessible and understandable to the public. This data should be available irrespective of the provider of the service and/or its commissioner or procurer. Prime accountability for public services should be either directly through an economic relationship between provider and user or through local government accountability </strong></em></p>
Yesterday's blog was a prime example of how <a href="http://wp.me/p14MGf-h2" target="_blank">NHS bureaucracy</a> only succeeds in baffling the public, hiding the facts and costing a fortune to operate.  It is hardly an easy model to follow in multi-agency shared services.
