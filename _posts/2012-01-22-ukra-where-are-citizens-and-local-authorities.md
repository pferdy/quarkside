Led by HMRC, we now have a <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/ukra1-0-lpa.doc" target="_blank">draft reference architecture for UK government services </a>(unclassified).  T<span class="MainBodyTextBold"><span style="font-weight:normal;">he purpose of this reference architecture is to:</span></span>
<ul style="margin-top:0;" type="disc">
	<li class="MsoNormal" style="margin-top:12pt;margin-bottom:14pt;"><em><strong><span class="MainBodyTextBold">Provide a framework to enable activity, process and complex systems to be broken down into small modular components, with known attributes, functionality, interfaces and dependencies.</span></strong></em></li>
	<li class="MsoNormal" style="margin-top:12pt;margin-bottom:14pt;"><em><strong><span class="MainBodyTextBold">Record those components in a way that business process owners, ICT service designers and solution providers can readily identify common areas of functionality.</span></strong></em></li>
	<li class="MsoNormal" style="margin-top:12pt;margin-bottom:14pt;"><em><strong><span class="MainBodyTextBold">Provide a framework against which common process and procedure can be developed to enable the effective reuse of solutions and services across government.</span></strong></em></li>
</ul>
It is not remotely citizen focussed - it is inward looking into arcane administrative activity.  Why?

One might also wonder what input there has been from the big service suppliers, Local Authorities (LAs).  Here's the list,  a working group was created, formally consisting of representatives from:
<ul>
	<li><em><strong>HM Revenue &amp; Customs (HMRC)</strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>Department for Work &amp; Pensions (DWP)</strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>Ministry of Justice (MoJ)</strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>Department Of Health (DoH)</strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>Home Office(HO)</strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>Ministry of Defence (MoD)</strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>Cabinet Office (CO)</strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>Department of Education (DoE)</strong></em></li>
</ul>
No sign of the Department for Communities and Local Government (DCLG) or LAs.  Why are LAs always latecomers into such strategic activity?

Let's hope that there will be some input from <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/03/16/shared-services-says-socitm-strategy/" target="_blank">Socitm</a> in due course, they are used to managing wide consultations in public sector ICT.
