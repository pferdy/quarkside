The <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/12/01/ig-time-the-spatio-temporal-paradigm/" target="_blank">Time</a> component of the <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/01/04/information-governance-defined/" target="_blank">seven dimensional information governance</a> (7DIG) framework was of little practical interest.  The spatio-temporal paradigm may hold the philosophical high ground, but a treatise on Gantt charts would have been out of place.  Serendipity has revealed a practical product, which is free, and is based on a philosophy of teams doing the right thing at the right time.  It brings together the <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/02/14/ig-people-huge-gap-in-skills/" target="_blank">People</a> who have to do the information governance with the <a title="IG Process: Due Diligence" href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/03/02/ig-process-due-diligence/" target="_blank">Process</a> that sets <a title="Objectives of IG" href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/02/08/objectives-of-ig/" target="_blank">Objectives</a> and uses <a title="IG Assets: Data Quality and ISO 8000" href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/12/01/ig-assets-data-quality-and-iso-8000/" target="_blank">Assets</a> to achieve the desired <a title="IG Outcomes: Focus on Benefits" href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/02/10/ig-outcomes-focus-on-benefits/" target="_blank">Outcomes</a>.  And it has a <a title="7DIG Risk Revisited: The Problem of Risk Matrices" href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/10/18/risk-revisited-the-problem-of-risk-matrices/" target="_blank">Risk</a> register.

It is project based – ideal for setting up an Information Governance process and should be adaptable for continuous monitoring. Look at some of the features:
<ol>
	<li>People from any department or supplier</li>
	<li>Assets, such as an information asset register and documentation, as a sharable resources</li>
	<li>Objectives, as part of the textual description including a breakdown into areas of interest or phases of work</li>
	<li>Process description in the form of tasks, which can be repeatable, taking us through the identification to deletion cycle</li>
	<li>Outcomes by the way of documented completion of tasks and compliance with targets</li>
	<li>Time charts, calendars, milestones and the possibility to export to your favourite Gantt chart tools</li>
	<li>Risk register, very simple to input and understand but good enough in most Information Governance régimes</li>
</ol>
The openness is especially encouraging.  It is completely Web-based and viewable from your iPod, iPhone or iPad.  Even the name is encouraging – <a href="http://www.teamworkpm.net/index.cfm" target="_blank">Teamwork</a>.

Quarkside wondered why such a simple product has not popped up in the radar previously.  Maybe it has only been promoted within the systems development community - and not yet reached public sector ICT managers.  Another job for <a href="http://www.socitm.gov.uk/" target="_blank">Socitm</a>?
