<a href="http://bit.ly/gVUqYT" target="_blank">DCLG has a large impact on local authorities</a>.  We expect some improvements (aka reductions) in reporting requirements and the signs look healthy.  For example the current SRP has:

<em><strong>1.3 Remove reporting burdens on local government from central departments </strong></em>
<ul>
	<li><em><strong>1.3.i. Abolish Comprehensive Area Assessment and cut local government inspection			(Completed) </strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>1.3.ii. Identify exceptional areas where central government needs to retain an oversight role			(Completed) </strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>1.3.iii. Develop a single, reduced, list of the data requirements placed on local government by central departments, working with other departments and local government		(In progress) </strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>1.3.iv. Develop and implement a process for managing new data requirements from departments, and from their associated inspectorates and regulators (Yet to start)</strong></em></li>
</ul>
Let's hope that DCLG also links up with the <a href="http://wp.me/14MGf" target="_blank">Cabinet Office SRP targets</a> for ICT.

If the <a href="http://bit.ly/i9gz89" target="_blank">Public Administration Select Committee (PASC)</a> study the inter-relationships between departments, agencies and local authorities, then they will surely find a complete lack of a coherent ICT policy.   Each department still seems to have an isolated approach to data management.  Interoperability between systems, or collaboration on shared services, is not on every department's menu.  They all need a common approach to <a title="Information Governance defined?" href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/01/04/information-governance-defined/" target="_blank">Information Governance</a>.
