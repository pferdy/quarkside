The demise of the <a href="http://wp.me/p14MGf-6Q" target="_blank">Id Card Project</a> in 2010 has not removed the growing need for trusted e-Identities (e-Ids) to give access to public sector services. The State benefits from lower administration costs and reduced fraud; Citizens benefit from much simpler and faster application for services. Far fewer errors will be propagated. The Cabinet Office solution is to encourage a market for Identity Provider (IdP) services from any number of accredited suppliers, many of whom should be from the private sector. Public Service Providers (PSPs) will trust the e-Ids from any such IdP. Their architecture diagram below has been largely unchanged for more than a year.

<a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/untitled-1.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-1837" title="IdHub" src="http://lenand.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/untitled-1.jpg" alt="Hub Architecture" width="421" height="258" /></a>

<span style="font-size:12pt;font-family:Arial;">Between the IdP and the PSP is the managed “Hub”.  This posting raises a fundamental question about why it is necessary.  There are already well established standards that control the governance requirements for federations of IdPs and PSPs.  <a href="http://openidentityexchange.org/sites/default/files/OIX%20Trust%20Framework%20Requirements.pdf" target="_blank">One is the OIX model</a>.  </span>

&nbsp;

<a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/untitled-2.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-1838" title="OIX Architecture" src="http://lenand.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/untitled-2.jpg" alt="OIX Architecture" width="338" height="248" /> </a>
<p class="MsoBodyText" style="margin-left:0;"><span>This standard does not have a central hub.<span>  </span>It has rules for level of assurance and protection.<span>  </span>It is supported by many international IdPs such as Google, Facebook and Microsoft.<span>  Public service organisations could act as both IdPs and relying parties.</span></span></p>
<p class="MsoBodyText" style="margin-left:0;"><span>The UK education sector uses a similar model for simplified sign on to multiple services.<span>  </span>Commonly known as Shibboleth, it is governed by the rules of the UK Federation.<span>  </span>It has an architecture that is scalable to millions of users without the need for a hub, see <a href="http://www.ukfederation.org.uk/">http://www.ukfederation.org.uk/</a>.<span>  </span>It is a governance issue, you either trust other members of a Federation, or you don’t.<span>  What are the problems of using such a federation architecture?  </span></span></p>
