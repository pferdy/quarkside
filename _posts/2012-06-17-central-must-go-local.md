<p style="padding-left:30px;"><em><strong>"With pressure to reduce spending, it is more important than ever that central government engages effectively with local government to draw on its expertise and capability in designing and delivering good quality, efficient public services."</strong></em></p>
 <a href="http://wp.me/6Pmf" target="_blank">The Great E-mancipator</a> led me to this quote from a National Audit Office report "<a href="http://www.nao.org.uk//idoc.ashx?docId=1be87873-30b5-49cb-b384-0745313645a9&amp;version=-1" target="_blank">Central government's communication and engagement with local government</a>".

They were not pulling punches when they wrote, " <em><strong>insufficient engagement with fire and rescue authorities was one factor that led to a major project to replace control rooms being cancelled in 2010. The project did not have the support of the majority of the end-users essential to its success, which wasted a minimum of £469 million</strong></em>".

DCLG are doing more now, but are other big central government departments actively improving communications and engagement?  What is the risk of similar wastage on Universal Credit, Individual Voter Registration and all things connected to e-Identities?  They should check their risk registers this week - and make sure they have enough LA engagement.
