The <a href="http://bit.ly/90dA3C">Cabinet Office new responsibilities and funding</a> will include: <em>"... £85m to support the introduction of individual electoral registration (IER) in 2014 to help tackle electoral fraud by moving away from household registration and confirming identities through secondary sources."</em>

In total, that sounds a reasonable amount of money, doesn't it?  Just to keep it in round numbers, that's about £200k per Local Authority (LA).   What does each council have to do for its money?  My random list:
<ul>
	<li>Re-vamp or re-write the current electoral registration software</li>
	<li>Introduce and communicate new processes, including for postal and proxy voters</li>
	<li>Verify identity in each LA by using 14 (or more) local and national databases by matching (or not) against the Electoral Register</li>
	<li>Identify people who are entitled to vote, but have not registered</li>
	<li>Prevent access to personal data from unauthorised people.</li>
</ul>
We will finish up with about 400 independent local registers of voters.  So far, so good (as long as you believe that any LA could perform all those non-trivial tasks for £200k).  One of my favourite tools of project management is the crystal ball.  It usually works.  However, if you don't feel comfortable telling your boss - the best view of the future can be seen by looking at what happened in the past.  The closest I can get to a comparison, from personal knowledge, is ContactPoint.

<a href="http://bit.ly/c6CT3t">ContactPoint (RIP)</a> created a single central data base, rather than 400 disconnected databases, but had otherwise comparable, but smaller, numbers:
<ul>
	<li>10 million citizens (vs 50 million),</li>
	<li>150 LAs (vs 400 LAs),</li>
	<li>Say, 1,500 system matches (vs 6,000 system matches)</li>
</ul>
A big difference was the original budget; £250m for ContactPoint, £85m for IER.  In other words, each IER LA will have about about one eighth of the budget (£200k vs £1.6m) for a project of greater dimensions and equally complex security issues.  IER has a timeframe of about 3 years.  ContactPoint consumed about 7 years - including a long hiccough for Security by Afterthought. As our American friends say, "Does it compute?"

My answer is - <strong>No</strong>. If you use the same architecture and development strategy.  Like a submarine, it will head for the rocks, and those on the bridge will panic - but not those who benefit from failed projects.

The answer is - <strong>Yes</strong>.  If you find a strategy that results in an order of magnitude improvement in the effectiveness of ICT.  I believe it may be achievable. Answers on one side of A4 should be sent to the Cabinet Office.  I am preparing mine.
