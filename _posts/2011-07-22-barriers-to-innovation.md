Innovation in institutions is counter-cultural, <a href="http://wp.me/p14MGf-20" target="_blank">especially in times of austerity</a>.  This was highlighted in a conversation with a supplier of a well established set of software tools.  "The department won't accept a more advanced analytical method - they only like rules based methods".

This actually exposes two barriers:
<ol>
	<li>Software suppliers do not promote innovation if it could impact a healthy revenue stream.</li>
	<li>Customers react against change if they do not understand an innovation.</li>
</ol>
Both barriers need to be overcome by customer leadership.
<ol>
	<li>They need to encourage the established suppliers to invest in research projects.</li>
	<li>They need to support schemes that reward innovative ideas for improving efficiency, however small.  This should develop a culture that embraces improvements.  Then, perhaps, they will relax their attachment to the constraints of institutionalised rules.</li>
</ol>
The reduction in public sector budgets should provide the incentive for leadership to connect to innovators.  They must adapt or disappear like dinosaurs.
