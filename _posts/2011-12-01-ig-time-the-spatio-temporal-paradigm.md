Time is one dimension of the spatio-temporal paradigm – which has an underlying principle:
<p style="padding-left:30px;"><em><strong>All Things exist in a universe of four dimensions, three of space and one of time.  </strong></em></p>
And a significant consequence:
<p style="padding-left:30px;"><em><strong>All Things in the past and future can be defined, in addition to all Things in the present.</strong></em></p>
Adopting the 4D paradigm is useful in information management:
<ul>
	<li>The state of a Thing can be defined by the time between a start event and an end event.</li>
	<li>Things can have many states at any point in time.</li>
	<li>Relationships can be defined between any Things, and such relationships also have time dimensions.</li>
</ul>
These can be applied in the domain of the <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/01/04/information-governance-defined/" target="_blank">7 Dimensional Information Governance framework (7DIG)</a>.  The seventh dimension considered, Time, is thus more than a clock and more than planning.  Time is one dimension of the Space-Time continuum; three dimensions of space and one of time.  7DIG framework is a model of concepts and the relationships between those concepts.  It is an ‘ontology’.  The Framework has attempted to develop a shared vocabulary and definition of concepts.  Unfortunately these concepts have been called dimensions and sub-dimensions.  In retrospect, another set of words may have been better to avoid possible confusion.

The 7DIG Time dimension applies to all the other six dimensions of the 7DIG framework ie <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/02/08/objectives-of-ig/" target="_blank">Objectives</a>, <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/02/23/ig-assets-value-and-protect/" target="_blank">Assets</a>, <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/02/14/ig-people-huge-gap-in-skills/" target="_blank">People</a>, <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/02/10/ig-outcomes-focus-on-benefits/" target="_blank">Outcomes</a>, <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/03/02/ig-process-due-diligence/" target="_blank">Process</a> and <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/10/18/risk-revisited-the-problem-of-risk-matrices/" target="_blank">Risk</a>.  Every single sub-dimension can have the question asked ‘When?’.
