At the end of May, the Cabinet Office reported that <a href="http://digital.cabinetoffice.gov.uk/2012/05/29/identity-assurance-goes-to-washington/" target="_blank">Identity Assurance goes to Washington</a>.  They seem to have taken heed of January's <a href="http://wp.me/p14MGf-tA" target="_blank">Quarkside support of the OIX standard for eIDs</a>.  This is the Open Identity Trust Framework (OITF) Model that does not require a central hub.  Perhaps the headline claim is a little strong, since there is no evidence that anybody there has read the blog!  Nevertheless, given that a central eID scheme has been ruled out by Government policy, it is a small step in the right direction.  Although a central scheme would be the most efficient to operate and implement, federation of eIDs is technically feasible.

Now for the next set of issues:
<ul>
	<li>Can the Government use a current implementation of OIX that prevents identity fraud, such as duplicate identities or impersonation?</li>
	<li>Will private sector identity providers, such as Google, provide eIDs at a price that makes commercial sense to themselves or citizens?</li>
	<li>Will the scheme be ready in time for <a href="http://digital.cabinetoffice.gov.uk/2012/05/25/identity-assurance-closer-to-market/" target="_blank">Universal Credit</a> with sufficient trust in electronic credentials?</li>
</ul>
With a risk manager's hat on the answers to all of these is probably "<strong>No</strong>", ie greater than 50% chance of missing targets.  Failure of Quality, failure of Cost and failure of Time; the fundamental triumvirate of project management.  Will this be another ill-fated YAGIF (Yet Another Government IT Failure) - which is actually a Governance failure, not ICT?

The OIX framework does not obviously include the high levels of trust that public sector agencies will need to dispense £billions with on-line transactions.  Something akin to an <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2012/06/12/7dig-identity-trust-matrix/" target="_blank">Identity Trust Matrix</a> may be necessary, tailored to the specific needs of service providers such as schools and the NHS.
