Some time ago, <a href="http://bit.ly/eX1UNb" target="_blank">Peter Cockrane</a> coined some "laws" of security.  Quarkside is happy to accept them at face value, and tried to categorise them into the three primary Quarks; Process, Governance and Technology:
<ol>
	<li>Resources are deployed inversely proportional to actual risk. (Governance)</li>
	<li>Perceived risk never equals actual risk. (Governance)</li>
	<li>Security people are never their own customer. (Process)</li>
	<li>Cracking systems is 100 times more fun than defending them.  (Technology)</li>
	<li>Security standards are an oxymoron. (Governance)</li>
	<li>There is always a threat.  The biggest threat always comes from a direction where you're not looking. (Process)</li>
	<li>You need two security groups - one to defend and one to attack. (Process)</li>
	<li>People expect 100% electronic security.  (Process)</li>
	<li>Nothing is 100% secure. (Technology)</li>
	<li>Security and operational requirements are mutually exclusive. (Governance)</li>
	<li>Hackers are smarter than you - they are younger! (Process)</li>
	<li>Legislation is (and will always be) 'greater than X' years behind. (Governance)</li>
	<li>As life becomes faster and more chaotic it becomes less secure - but the good news is half-lives are getting shorter too. (Perplexity)</li>
	<li>People are always the biggest risk factor. Machines are perverse, but they aren't devious... yet! (Governance)</li>
</ol>
Apart from Law 13, it seemed to work.   Can anybody help?

What is not apparent is that the list is Collectively Exhaustive, and they may not be Mutually Exclusive.  <a href="http://wp.me/P14MGf-2" target="_blank">Quarkside</a> is not yet ready to devise a MECE list, but it would start top-down and try to avoid both gaps and overlaps.
