Let's start at the beginning.  The  <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/01/pascquest2.pdf" target="_blank">Public Administration Select Committee (PASC)</a> asked for evidence with 12 questions, the first being:

<em><strong>1. How well is technology policy co-ordinated across Government?</strong></em>

It is not obvious that technology policy is coordinated.  How much work done by various CIO and CTO Council working parties has been completed and published?   One example is the “<a href="http://bit.ly/i9PxeH" target="_blank">Enterprise Architecture for UK Government</a>”, started in 2006, and already featured in a previous <a href="http://wp.me/p14MGf-6G" target="_blank">blog</a>.  It was a brave attempt to develop an Enterprise Architecture reference model, intended to:

“<em><strong>identify opportunities that will underpin improvements in the areas focussed on in the strategy, in particular: </strong></em>
<ul>
	<li><em><strong>Services enabled by IT designed around citizens and businesses </strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>Moving to a shared services approach </strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>Broadening and deepening government’s professionalism”</strong></em></li>
</ul>
Benefits looked very promising:
<ul>
	<li><em><strong>"Promoting the development of common infrastructure </strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>Improved management of risk </strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>Identifying and aggregating demand to promote efficient use of resources </strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>Sustainable alignment of business and IT functions </strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>Agreement of shared standards to promote better inter-working between agencies </strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>A greater competition in the supply of IT services and products </strong></em></li>
	<li><em><strong>Improved business agility and a reduction of total cost of ownership</strong></em>"</li>
</ul>
So many questions could be asked:
<ul>
	<li>Where are the results?</li>
	<li>How much budget was consumed?</li>
	<li>Who benefitted?</li>
	<li>What was learnt?</li>
	<li>Why was the effort stopped?</li>
</ul>
There was lots of good will from both local and central government ICT managers, but no programme leadership.  It was not a failure of policy; it was a failure of governance.  And, as Quarkside knows – <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/about/" target="_blank">Governance Matters</a>.
