The Ministry of Justice has called for <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/02/evidencecall.pdf" target="_blank">evidence on the EU Data Protection Proposals</a>.  They are seeking the views of "<em><strong>data controllers and data processors, rights groups and information policy experts or anyone with a professional or personal interest in data protection</strong></em>".  Quite right - they have asked over 150 organisations to answer a lengthy questionnaire.  They have selected an eclectic mix of companies from Abbey Quilting Limited to Yahoo!  Many are understandable, such as No2ID and the Information Commissioner’s Office.  It is the omissions that are mysterious:
<p style="padding-left:30px;">- Virtually no central government departments have been asked.  One would have expected HMRC and DWP should have some evidence - not just the DVLA.</p>
<p style="padding-left:30px;">- No representative organisation from local government, such as Solace, LGA or Socitm.  But there are a few individual local authorities such as Norfolk County Council.</p>
When these organisations collectively record personal data for every single citizen, surely they should have been asked.  It is not an issue of politeness - but politics and policy.  Information governance must include the key stakeholders - not just an apparently random selection.
