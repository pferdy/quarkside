Whilst reviewing a worthy document on leadership in ICT, it seemed to lack structure.  The contents were fine, but left an uneasy feeling that not everything had been covered.  Was the advice <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MECE_principle" target="_blank">MECE</a>?  This led to a Leadership Model, reminiscent of one used by Quarkside <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2011/01/04/information-governance-defined/" target="_blank">before</a> (7DIG).  The seven primary dimensions were Direction, Stakeholders, Resources, Action, Outcomes, Time and Risk.

The Expanded Leadership Model is probably a reasonable Framework for many ICT organisations.

<a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/leadership1.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-1719" title="Leadership" src="http://lenand.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/leadership1.jpg" alt="" width="631" height="444" /></a>

It triggered an idea of a Framework for Frameworks. A self-help tool that gives a structure for all MECE dimensions.  It starts with seven generic concepts.
<ol>
	<li>Context:  the business environment and constraints</li>
	<li>Subjects:  <strong>People</strong> who are involved in processes</li>
	<li>Verbs: Words that denote <strong>action</strong> by people, <strong>Process</strong></li>
	<li>Objects:  <strong>Things</strong> that are resources to be consumed or created</li>
	<li>Outcomes: Desired results from the process actions</li>
	<li>Time:  When things are to be done</li>
	<li>Risk:  What can disrupt the Process - and how to manage risk</li>
</ol>
The diagram may help some people.  Let us know if it has helped you.

<a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/framework.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-1717" title="Framework" src="http://lenand.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/framework.jpg" alt="" width="631" height="444" /></a>

Let the organisation decide the next level down.  They know the trigger points and politics that will enable changed behaviour or transformation of processes.  For many purposes, this simple framework for frameworks should be more effective than <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKinsey_7S_Framework" target="_blank">McKinsey's famous 7S framework </a>for changing organisations.
