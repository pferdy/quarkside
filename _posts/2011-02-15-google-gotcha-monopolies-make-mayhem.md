Google has been a favourite company for many years.  Is it now behaving too much like the Microsoft monopoly.

Like Microsoft and their ridiculous ribbons, Google Maps has got worse:
<ul>
	<li>They have removed some handy buttons on a toolbar for toggling between optional views and facilities.</li>
	<li>They have provide a useless set of floating boxes (large) that obscure the map.</li>
	<li>The blue route maps have disappeared.</li>
	<li>It is much less useful than before and there is no option to revert to the perfectly adequate older system.</li>
	<li>There is no way of communicating with Google to express objections or get support.</li>
	<li>It seems as though they have got an excess of developers with whizzy ideas, time on their hands and no concept of how to maintain customer satisfaction.</li>
</ul>
Computer Weekly also informs us that <a href="http://bit.ly/hOG0sC" target="_blank">Google are moving to 2 factor authentication</a>.  Let people have the choice.  Let people understand the risk and take a personal decision about whether to stick with a single factor.  Perhaps they do not understand that some people do not live by mobile phone wherever they go.

With so many people using Google Applications, they are becoming a de facto standard with enormous marketing power.  There is a greater need for ensuring that previous implementations are compatible.  Look at the havoc caused by different versions of MS Office not interoperating sensibly.  Look at the <a href="http://bit.ly/fxejnA" target="_blank">high cost of IE 9 now following the international standard</a> and ceasing to support earlier deviations.

Monopolies need scrutiny and governance.
