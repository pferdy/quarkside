The buzz at the <a href="http://www.meetup.com/iotlondon/events/172121102/?comment_table_id=170182012&amp;comment_table_name=reply" target="_blank">IoTLondon </a>showcase demonstrated the huge energy of entrepreneurs.  Kemuri was able to demonstrate tangible progress since the <a title="Kemuri:  Benefits First" href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/2014/02/19/kemuri-benefits-first/" target="_blank">original presentation</a> in February.  The strapline was a bit tacky, "Granny Monitor", but all better suggestions are welcome.

People were interested in poking their fingers into the working demonstrator, thankfully it was in battery powered mode and not connected to the mains.  It contains:
<ul>
	<li>Temperature sensor - for hypothermia risk</li>
	<li>Power sensor - for dehydration or nutritional risk</li>
	<li>Motion sensor - for immobility or fall risk</li>
	<li>Controller board - to schedule data transmissions</li>
	<li>Communications board and aerial</li>
	<li>Power socket</li>
</ul>
All fitting into a UK standard double socket enclosure.  Mission accomplished.

We had genuine enquiries about selling to the general public.  The answer has to be "No".  The equipment must be professionally installed and the first units will go to telecare companies or care agencies.  Furthermore, it was just a demonstrator and it has to go into a prototype, certification and pilot phase before it should be introduced at scale.

As pointed out in the Marketing <a href="http://lenand.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/fly140409d.pdf">Flyer</a>, we are "seeking supporters, collaborators and funders" before taking the next steps.  Tell your friends.
