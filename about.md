<strong>Interoperability Matters</strong>

----------------------------

Three quarks for Muster Mark!
Sure he has not got much of a bark
And sure any he has it's all beside the mark.

— James Joyce, Finnegans Wake

-----------------------------

A <a title="Quark" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark#Table_of_properties">quark</a> is an elementary particle and a fundamental constituent of matter.  Protons and neutrons are both composed of three quarks - and they must have different colour.  Quarkside uses the idea of a triple: three domains, each with its own specialists, each essential, and none sufficient on its own:
<ul>
	<li>Process:  What is needed?</li>
	<li>Governance:  How is it controlled?</li>
	<li>Technology:  How is it to be done?</li>
</ul>
Each domain has its own language, easily misunderstood in the others.  Interoperability is what happens when they combine: organisations, systems and people exchanging meaning, not just data.

The Quarkside strap line is "Quarks combine to make matter; ideas combine to make systems".  It aims to bring knowledge from all three domains together and to expose these ideas to the organisations that depend on services working across boundaries.  Government departments, local government, police, health, fire &amp; rescue, other public sector agencies and the voluntary sector all need their systems to join up, with each other and increasingly across Europe.

Let's see if we can influence thinking that achieves people working together.
